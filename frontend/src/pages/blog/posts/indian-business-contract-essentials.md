Whether you are a solo freelancer, a startup founder or the legal head at a mid-size company, every commercial relationship in India should be backed by a well-drafted contract. A handshake may feel faster, but it offers zero protection when a client delays payment, a vendor delivers sub-standard work, or a partner decides to walk away with your IP.

This guide covers everything an Indian business needs to know about drafting, reviewing, and enforcing contracts in 2026 — from the essential clauses to stamp duty requirements and the latest e-signature rules.

> This guide provides general information, not legal advice. For high-value or complex agreements, have a qualified lawyer review the final draft.

## Why Every Indian Business Needs Proper Contracts

Indian courts are overloaded — a commercial dispute can take 5 to 15 years to resolve through litigation. A well-drafted contract is your first line of defence because it:

- **Defines obligations clearly** so both parties know exactly what is expected.
- **Reduces ambiguity** that leads to disputes in the first place.
- **Provides enforceability** — under the Indian Contract Act, 1872, a valid written agreement is binding and enforceable.
- **Enables faster dispute resolution** through arbitration or mediation clauses.
- **Protects your IP** with confidentiality and assignment clauses.
- **Satisfies compliance** — many regulatory frameworks (GST, DPDPA, labour laws) require documented agreements.

## Types of Business Contracts in India

Indian businesses commonly need these categories of agreements:

### 1. Service Agreements
Used when one party provides professional or technical services to another. Covers scope, deliverables, timelines, payment milestones and service level expectations.

### 2. Employment Contracts
Governed by various labour laws including the Industrial Disputes Act, Shops and Establishments Acts, and the new Labour Codes. Must cover compensation, working hours, leave, termination notice, confidentiality and non-solicitation.

### 3. Partnership Deeds
Essential for any partnership firm registered under the Indian Partnership Act, 1932. Covers profit sharing, capital contribution, roles, decision-making authority and dissolution terms.

### 4. Vendor and Supply Agreements
Cover procurement of goods or raw materials. Should address pricing, delivery timelines, quality standards, rejection procedures, indemnity and force majeure.

### 5. Non-Disclosure Agreements (NDAs)
Protect confidential information exchanged between parties. Critical before investor pitches, hiring discussions, technology transfers and M&A due diligence. Our [NDA generator](/tools/nda-generator) can create one in under a minute.

### 6. Consulting and Freelancer Agreements
Govern independent contractor relationships. Must clearly establish that the consultant is not an employee — important for tax (TDS under Section 194J) and labour law compliance.

### 7. Rental and Lease Agreements
Governed by state-specific Rent Control Acts. Stamp duty and registration requirements vary by state — use our [stamp duty calculator](/tools/stamp-duty-calculator) to check.

### 8. Loan Agreements
Cover terms of lending between businesses or individuals. Must specify principal, interest rate, repayment schedule, security/collateral and default provisions.

## Essential Clauses Every Indian Contract Must Have

No matter the type of agreement, these clauses form the backbone of any enforceable Indian business contract. You can find ready-to-use versions of each in our [clause library](/tools/clause-library).

### 1. Parties and Recitals
Identify each party with full legal name, address, registration number (CIN/GSTIN where applicable) and authorised signatory. The recitals section provides context — why the parties are entering the agreement.

### 2. Scope of Work / Obligations
The most important operational clause. Define deliverables, timelines, quality standards and acceptance criteria in specific, measurable terms. Vague scope is the number one cause of contract disputes.

### 3. Consideration and Payment Terms
Under Section 10 of the Indian Contract Act, lawful consideration is a requirement for a valid contract. Specify the total amount, payment schedule (milestone-based, monthly, on delivery), currency, GST treatment and late payment penalties.

### 4. Term and Termination
Define the contract's duration, renewal mechanism (auto-renew or manual) and termination rights. Include both termination for convenience (with notice period) and termination for cause (material breach, insolvency).

### 5. Confidentiality
Obligate both parties to protect sensitive information. Define what constitutes confidential information, the permitted uses, standard exclusions (publicly available, independently developed) and the survival period after termination.

### 6. Indemnification
Allocate risk by requiring one or both parties to compensate the other for losses caused by their breach, negligence or IP infringement. Consider adding a liability cap to limit exposure.

### 7. Limitation of Liability
Cap the maximum financial exposure under the contract — typically tied to fees paid in the preceding 12 months. Exclude indirect, consequential and punitive damages. Carve out exceptions for indemnity, confidentiality breaches and gross negligence.

### 8. Dispute Resolution
Specify the mechanism: arbitration (recommended for speed and privacy under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996), mediation, or court litigation. Name the seat, language and governing rules.

### 9. Governing Law and Jurisdiction
State that Indian law governs the agreement and name the city whose courts have exclusive jurisdiction. This is critical for cross-border contracts.

### 10. Force Majeure
Excuse non-performance due to events beyond reasonable control — natural disasters, pandemics, government orders, war. Include notification obligations, mitigation requirements and a termination right for extended events.

## Indian Contract Act 1872 — Key Provisions

The Indian Contract Act is the foundational legislation governing agreements in India. Every business should understand these sections:

### Section 10 — What Makes a Contract Valid
A valid contract requires: (1) free consent of parties competent to contract, (2) lawful consideration, (3) lawful object, and (4) the agreement must not be expressly declared void.

### Section 23 — Unlawful Consideration and Object
An agreement is void if its consideration or object is forbidden by law, defeats the provisions of any law, involves fraud, causes injury, or is immoral or opposed to public policy.

### Section 27 — Restraint of Trade
Every agreement in restraint of the trade of any person is void to that extent. This is why post-employment non-compete clauses are largely unenforceable in India — courts consistently strike them down. Non-solicitation clauses with reasonable scope are a better alternative.

### Section 56 — Frustration of Contract (Doctrine of Frustration)
If an act becomes impossible or unlawful after the contract is made (due to events the promisor could not prevent), the agreement becomes void. COVID-19 litigation extensively tested this provision.

### Sections 73–74 — Damages and Penalties
Section 73 allows recovery of damages naturally arising from breach. Section 74 governs liquidated damages — Indian courts can reduce penalty clauses to "reasonable compensation" even if a specific amount is agreed upon.

## Stamp Duty and Registration Requirements

Stamp duty is a state-levied tax on legal documents. Failing to pay stamp duty makes a contract inadmissible as evidence in court.

### Key Points
- **Rates vary by state** — Maharashtra, Karnataka, Delhi and others each have different schedules.
- **Common agreements requiring stamp duty**: rental/lease agreements, partnership deeds, loan agreements, sale deeds, employment bonds.
- **E-stamping** is available in most states through the Stock Holding Corporation of India (SHCIL) platform.
- **Registration** is mandatory for certain documents under the Registration Act, 1908 — particularly leases exceeding 12 months and immovable property transfers.

## Digital Contracts and E-Signatures

The Information Technology Act, 2000 provides the legal framework for electronic contracts and signatures in India.

### Section 5 — Legal Recognition of E-Signatures
Electronic signatures have the same legal standing as handwritten signatures, provided they are affixed in the manner prescribed by law.

### Types of E-Signatures Recognised
1. **Digital Signatures** (Section 3) — Based on asymmetric cryptography, issued by licensed Certifying Authorities. Highest legal validity.
2. **Aadhaar e-Sign** — OTP-based signing using Aadhaar authentication. Valid for most commercial contracts.
3. **Electronic Signatures** (Section 3A) — Broader category covering reliable electronic methods specified by the government.

### Exceptions — What Cannot Be Signed Electronically
- Negotiable instruments (cheques, promissory notes)
- Powers of attorney
- Wills and testamentary dispositions
- Trust deeds
- Contracts for sale or conveyance of immovable property

## Common Mistakes Indian Businesses Make

### 1. No Written Contract at All
The most common and most dangerous mistake. Verbal agreements are technically valid under Indian law but nearly impossible to enforce.

### 2. Vague Scope of Work
"The vendor will provide IT services" is not a scope. Define deliverables, timelines, acceptance criteria and quality standards in measurable terms.

### 3. Missing or Inadequate Dispute Resolution
Without an arbitration clause, you are stuck with court litigation — which can take years. Always include a clear arbitration mechanism.

### 4. Ignoring Stamp Duty
An unstamped contract is inadmissible as evidence in Indian courts. Even if the underlying agreement is valid, you cannot enforce it through litigation.

### 5. Using Templates Without Customisation
Generic templates from the internet may not reflect Indian law or your specific business context. Always customise templates with the correct governing law, jurisdiction and compliance requirements.

### 6. No Limitation of Liability
Without a liability cap, a minor project could expose your company to unlimited damages. Always include a mutual limitation of liability clause.

### 7. Unenforceable Non-Compete Clauses
Post-employment non-compete clauses are void under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act. Use non-solicitation clauses instead — they are more likely to be upheld.

### 8. Forgetting Data Protection
With the Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA) 2023 now in effect, any contract involving personal data must include data processing, security and breach notification provisions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a contract valid without stamp duty in India?
The contract itself may be valid as an agreement between parties, but it is inadmissible as evidence in any Indian court without proper stamp duty. This effectively makes it unenforceable through litigation. You can pay stamp duty with a penalty even after execution — check the applicable rates with our [stamp duty calculator](/tools/stamp-duty-calculator).

### Can I draft my own business contract without a lawyer?
Yes, for straightforward agreements. Tools like [DoAide Contracts](/generator) provide legally structured templates that cover the essential clauses. However, for high-value deals (above INR 50 lakhs), complex IP arrangements, cross-border agreements or regulated industries, engaging a lawyer is strongly recommended.

### What is the difference between an agreement and a contract?
Under Indian law, every contract is an agreement, but not every agreement is a contract. An agreement becomes a contract when it satisfies the requirements of Section 10 of the Indian Contract Act — free consent, competent parties, lawful consideration and lawful object. A social promise between friends is an agreement but not a contract.

### Are digital contracts legally binding in India?
Yes. The Information Technology Act, 2000 (Section 10A) specifically provides that contracts formed through electronic means are valid and enforceable. The key requirement is that both parties must have access to the electronic record and the ability to retain it.

### How long should I keep signed contracts?
The Limitation Act, 1963 prescribes different periods for different types of claims — generally 3 years for most contract disputes, but up to 12 years for suits on registered instruments. As a best practice, retain executed contracts for at least 8 years after expiry or termination, or as required by industry-specific regulations.

### What happens if a contract clause is found invalid?
If your contract includes a severability clause (and it should), the invalid provision is severed and the remaining clauses continue in full force. Without a severability clause, there is a risk that the entire contract could be voided.
