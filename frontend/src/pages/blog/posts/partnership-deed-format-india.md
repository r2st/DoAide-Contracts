Two friends start a business. They split the work, share the profit, and agree on everything until they don't. A clear **partnership deed** is the single best way to prevent the fights that end many Indian small businesses: who gets how much profit, who can sign cheques, and what happens if someone wants to leave.

This guide explains the **partnership deed format in India**, its must-have clauses, stamp duty, registration under the Indian Partnership Act, 1932, and how startup co-founders should think about a co-founder agreement. ## What is a partnership deed?

A partnership deed (sometimes called a partnership agreement, or *sajhedari anubandh* in Hindi) is a written agreement between two or more people who agree to carry on a business together and share its profits. It's governed by the **Indian Partnership Act, 1932**.

A partnership can legally exist on an oral agreement, but in practice you need a written deed to:

- open a current bank account in the firm's name,
- get a PAN and GST registration for the firm,
- register the firm with the Registrar of Firms, and
- avoid disputes about profit share, capital and exit.

## Mandatory and important clauses

| Clause | What it covers |
|---|---|
| Name and address of the firm | Trade name and principal place of business |
| Partners' details | Names, addresses, ages, PAN |
| Nature of business | What the firm will do, broad enough to allow growth |
| Commencement and duration | Start date; "partnership at will" or a fixed term |
| Capital contribution | How much each partner brings, in cash or kind |
| Profit and loss sharing ratio | E.g. 50:50 or 60:40. If not stated, the Act presumes equal shares |
| Interest on capital and drawings | Rate, if any, within income-tax limits |
| Partner remuneration | Salaries to working partners, within the limits allowed under the Income-tax Act |
| Duties and powers | Who manages operations, finance and signing authority |
| Bank account operation | Single or joint signatures, and limits |
| Admission of new partners | Process and consent required |
| Retirement and death | Notice period, valuation of share, payout timeline |
| Dissolution | Grounds and settlement of accounts |
| Non-compete and confidentiality | Within what Indian law allows |
| Dispute resolution | Arbitration seat, governing law |

Remuneration and interest clauses matter for tax as well: they must be authorised by the deed to be deductible for the firm, so take a CA's advice on the wording.

## Partnership deed stamp duty

A partnership deed must be executed on non-judicial stamp paper of the value prescribed by your state. The duty often depends on the capital contributed and can be a fixed amount or a slab. Check your state's rate with the [stamp duty calculator](/tools/stamp-duty-calculator) and read our [stamp duty guide](/blog/stamp-duty-guide-india).

Notarisation is common practice and is often asked for by banks, but it doesn't replace proper stamping.

## Should you register the partnership firm?

Registration with the state's Registrar of Firms is **optional**, but an unregistered firm faces real disadvantages under **Section 69** of the Partnership Act:

- The firm cannot file a suit against third parties to enforce a contract.
- A partner cannot sue the firm or other partners to enforce rights arising from the deed.

So most firms that deal with customers and vendors register. The typical process is to file the prescribed form with the deed, partners' ID and address proof, and proof of the business address, then pay the state fee. Many states now accept applications online.

## Partnership firm vs LLP vs private limited

| | Partnership firm | LLP | Private limited company |
|---|---|---|---|
| Law | Partnership Act, 1932 | LLP Act, 2008 | Companies Act, 2013 |
| Liability | Unlimited, personal | Limited to contribution | Limited to shares |
| Founding document | Partnership deed | LLP agreement | MoA, AoA + shareholders' agreement |
| Compliance | Low | Moderate (annual MCA filings) | Highest |
| Fundraising | Difficult | Limited | Easiest (equity) |

If you plan to raise investment, a private limited company with a **co-founder or shareholders' agreement** is usually the better choice.

## Co-founder agreement basics for startups

For startups structured as private limited companies, the equivalent of a partnership deed is a co-founder agreement (often folded into a shareholders' agreement). Key points:

1. **Equity split** and the reasoning behind it.
2. **Vesting:** founders' shares vest over time (often 4 years with a 1-year cliff), so someone who leaves early doesn't walk away with a large stake.
3. **Roles and decision-making:** who is CEO, who has final say on product and on hiring.
4. **IP assignment:** all code, designs and trademarks belong to the company, including work done before incorporation.
5. **Full-time commitment** and limits on outside work.
6. **Exit and buy-back:** what happens to unvested and vested shares when a founder leaves.
7. **Deadlock resolution:** a mediator, an advisor's casting vote, or a buy-sell mechanism.
8. **Confidentiality** and non-solicitation of employees and customers.

## Sample partnership deed outline

```
PARTNERSHIP DEED

This deed of partnership is made on [date] at [city] between:
1. [Partner 1 name, age, address, PAN] ("First Partner")
2. [Partner 2 name, age, address, PAN] ("Second Partner")

1. Name of firm: M/s [Firm Name]
2. Principal place of business: [address]
3. Business: [nature of business]
4. Commencement: [date]; Duration: at will
5. Capital: First Partner ₹[ ], Second Partner ₹[ ]
6. Profit/loss sharing ratio: [50:50]
7. Interest on capital: [ ]% p.a. (subject to Income-tax Act limits)
8. Remuneration to working partners: as permitted under the Income-tax Act
9. Banking: account operated by [any one / jointly]
10. Retirement: [3] months' written notice
11. Disputes: arbitration under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996

Signed by partners in the presence of two witnesses.
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is a partnership deed mandatory in India?

The Partnership Act does not strictly require a written deed, but in practice you need one to open a bank account, get a PAN and GST registration for the firm, register the firm, and avoid disputes between partners.

### Is registration of a partnership firm compulsory?

No, registration is optional. However, under Section 69 of the Indian Partnership Act, an unregistered firm cannot sue third parties to enforce contracts, and partners cannot sue the firm or each other to enforce rights under the deed. Most firms therefore register.

### What is the stamp duty on a partnership deed?

It is set by each state's Stamp Act and often depends on the capital contributed. Check your state's current rates or use a stamp duty calculator before buying stamp paper.

### What happens if the profit-sharing ratio is not mentioned in the deed?

Under the Indian Partnership Act, if the deed is silent, partners share profits and losses equally. Always state the ratio explicitly to avoid disputes.

### Do startup co-founders need a partnership deed?

If the startup is a private limited company, co-founders need a co-founder or shareholders' agreement rather than a partnership deed. It should cover equity, vesting, roles, IP assignment and exit terms.
