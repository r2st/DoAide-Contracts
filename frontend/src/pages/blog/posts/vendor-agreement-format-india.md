Whether you run a restaurant buying vegetables daily, a D2C brand sourcing packaging, or an IT company hiring a hardware supplier, your vendors can make or break your business. A written **vendor agreement** sets expectations on price, quality, delivery and payment, so that a late shipment or a disputed invoice doesn't turn into a costly fight.

This guide explains the essential **vendor agreement format in India**, the clauses that matter most, and India-specific points such as GST, TDS and MSME payment timelines. You can create a draft instantly with the [free vendor agreement template](/template/vendor-agreement).

> General information only, not legal or tax advice. Consult a professional for high-value or complex contracts.

## Vendor agreement vs purchase order

Many businesses buy using purchase orders (POs) alone. That works for one-off buys, but for ongoing supply a **master vendor agreement** plus individual POs is far better:

| | Purchase order only | Master vendor agreement + POs |
|---|---|---|
| Covers | One transaction | The full relationship |
| Quality standards | Often missing | Clearly defined |
| Penalties and liability | Rarely covered | Agreed in advance |
| Termination | Unclear | Defined notice and exit |
| Negotiation effort | Every time | Once |

## Essential clauses in a vendor agreement

### 1. Parties and scope

Legal names, addresses, GSTINs and PANs of both parties. Describe the goods or services clearly, with specifications attached as a schedule.

### 2. Pricing and price revision

Fixed prices, a rate card, or a formula linked to raw material prices. State whether prices include GST, freight, packing and insurance, and how often prices can be revised.

### 3. Orders and delivery

How orders are placed (PO or email), lead times, delivery location, and who bears transport risk. Use Incoterms-style clarity: "delivered to our warehouse, unloaded", not just "delivery included".

### 4. Quality, inspection and returns

Quality standards (BIS, FSSAI or your own specifications), the inspection window after delivery, and the procedure for rejecting and replacing defective goods.

### 5. Payment terms

Credit period, invoice requirements, and payment method. Two Indian rules matter here:

- **MSME vendors:** under the MSMED Act, 2006, buyers must pay registered micro and small enterprises within the agreed period, which cannot exceed **45 days** from acceptance. Delayed payments attract compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate.
- **Income-tax Section 43B(h):** for buyers, payments due to micro and small enterprises that are not made within the MSMED Act time limit may not be deductible as an expense until they are actually paid.

Ask vendors for their Udyam registration so you know which rules apply. If you're the supplier, you can estimate interest on overdue invoices with the [DoAide late fee calculator](https://invoicer.doaide.com/tools/late-fee).

### 6. GST and TDS

- The vendor must issue a valid GST tax invoice, file returns on time and ensure the invoice shows up in your GSTR-2B, so you can claim input tax credit.
- Include a clause requiring the vendor to compensate you if ITC is denied because of their non-compliance.
- Deduct TDS where applicable (for example under Section 194C for contract work or Section 194Q for high-value purchases of goods) and issue TDS certificates.

### 7. Service levels and penalties

For recurring supply or services, define measurable targets (on-time delivery percentage, defect rate, response time) and the consequences of missing them, such as service credits or liquidated damages. Under Section 74 of the Indian Contract Act, courts may reduce penalties to reasonable compensation, so keep them proportionate. See our [SLA guide](/blog/sla-template-indian-it-companies).

### 8. Warranties and indemnity

The vendor warrants that goods are fit for purpose, meet specifications and don't infringe third-party IP, and indemnifies you against losses from breaches, defects and legal non-compliance.

### 9. Limitation of liability

A cap on each party's liability (often linked to the contract value) and exclusion of indirect losses. Make sure the cap doesn't wipe out your indemnity protection.

### 10. Confidentiality

Protect pricing, designs and customer information. For sensitive projects, sign a separate [NDA](/tools/nda-generator).

### 11. Term and termination

Duration, renewal, termination for convenience (with notice), and termination for breach (with a cure period). Spell out what happens to pending orders and payments.

### 12. Force majeure

Events beyond control (natural disasters, government lockdowns, strikes) that suspend obligations, plus notice requirements and a long-stop termination date.

### 13. Dispute resolution

Negotiation, then mediation or arbitration under the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, with a named seat (for example, Mumbai or Delhi) and governing law.

## Vendor agreement checklist

- [ ] Correct legal names, GSTINs and PANs
- [ ] Specifications attached as a schedule
- [ ] Prices, taxes and freight clearly stated
- [ ] Delivery terms and risk transfer defined
- [ ] Inspection and rejection procedure
- [ ] Payment terms compliant with the MSMED Act where applicable
- [ ] GST/ITC protection clause and TDS
- [ ] SLAs and proportionate penalties
- [ ] Liability cap and indemnity
- [ ] Termination and force majeure
- [ ] Stamped as per state law, signed by authorised signatories

## Stamp duty

Commercial agreements must be stamped according to your state's Stamp Act. Unstamped or under-stamped agreements can be impounded and are not admissible as evidence until the duty and penalty are paid. Check with the [stamp duty calculator](/tools/stamp-duty-calculator).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should a vendor agreement include?

At minimum: parties, scope and specifications, pricing, delivery terms, quality and inspection, payment terms, GST and TDS responsibilities, warranties, indemnity, liability cap, confidentiality, termination, force majeure and dispute resolution.

### What is the maximum payment period for MSME suppliers?

Under the MSMED Act, 2006, buyers must pay registered micro and small enterprise suppliers within the agreed period, which cannot exceed 45 days from the day of acceptance. Late payment attracts compound interest at three times the RBI bank rate.

### Does a vendor agreement need stamp paper?

Yes. Agreements in India should be stamped as per the applicable state Stamp Act, either on physical stamp paper or via e-stamping. An unstamped agreement is not admissible as evidence until the duty and penalty are paid.

### Is a purchase order enough without a vendor agreement?

For one-off purchases, a detailed PO with standard terms may be enough. For ongoing supply, a master vendor agreement gives far better protection on quality, liability, penalties and termination.

### How do I protect my GST input tax credit in a vendor agreement?

Require the vendor to issue valid tax invoices, file GST returns on time and ensure invoices appear in your GSTR-2B. Add a clause allowing you to withhold payment or recover any ITC lost due to the vendor's non-compliance.
